# Lumen Chamber · six-scene board

Original concept, not a real product claim. Spatial blocking specimen, not a photoreal production master.

| Beat | Purpose | Semantic meaning | Visitor invitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · arrival | Establish the chamber | A small suspended optical engine belongs to a larger room. | Scroll or select a chapter to approach. |
| 2 · approach | Find the mechanism | The camera approaches a shared optical axis. | A lens is a mechanism, not a decoration. |
| 3 · construction | Reveal the stack | Separate the shell, iris and light plane along their shared axis. | Inspect construction without losing the original object. |
| 4 · participate | Shape the beam | Aperture input changes the opening, beam and environment. | Change the aperture or finish with the controls. |
| 5 · threshold | Cross the frame | The aperture grows past the viewport while its light becomes the environment. | The viewport is a threshold, not a picture frame. |
| 6 · resolve | Return to utility | The complete object returns with the chosen configuration intact. | Keep your selection; reset or return to the normal document. |

The instrument keeps a shared axis and stable configuration. The beam changes the room,
then the lens passes the apparent screen frame. This demonstrates a narrative relationship
beyond turning an isolated model. Hold states and direct chapter access remain available.

## Alternate treatments
A static technical editorial would communicate construction efficiently but lose causal light
response and threshold staging. A live mesh renderer could improve material/occlusion fidelity
and free view; it costs native source, asset export and target-GPU qualification. The shipped
SVG/projection route proves story-state and input affordances without claiming those native/GPU
qualities. Replace only the renderer when those qualities are justified; retain the meaning.
